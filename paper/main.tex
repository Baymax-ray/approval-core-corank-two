\begin{abstract}
Whether every approval-based committee election admits a deterministic
committee in the Hare core remains open.  We prove nonemptiness on the full
co-rank-two domain: every voter may disapprove at most two candidates, while
the numbers of candidates, seats, and voter types are otherwise unrestricted.
The proof gives an explicit selection rule.  Among all missing sets of the
required size, first maximize the weight of two-disapproval voters whose
disapproval pair is hit, and then maximize total disapproval incidence.  A
random-set inequality rules out targets one seat below the committee, while
the secondary tie-break rules out unanimous equal-size targets.  Smaller
targets cannot improve any voter.  Two independent exact implementations
enumerate every target for more than two hundred thousand maximizing
committees on complementary finite grids.  These computations audit, but are
not needed for, the unbounded symbolic theorem.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An approval election consists of a finite voter set $\N$, a candidate set
$C$ of size $m$, approval sets $A_i\subseteq C$, and a target committee size
$k$.  A nonempty set $T\subseteq C$ blocks a size-$k$ committee $W$ at the
Hare quota when
\[
  \sum_{i:\,|A_i\cap T|>|A_i\cap W|} w_i\ge \frac{|T|}{k},
  \tag{1}
\]
where the positive rational voter weights sum to one.  A committee is in the
Hare core if it has no blocker.

The core was introduced as a strong proportional-representation requirement
for approval committees by Aziz et al.~\cite{aziz2017}.  General deterministic
nonemptiness is open.  The current parameter frontiers include $k\le8$ and
$m\le15$~\cite{peters2025}, and at most seven weighted voter
types~\cite{becker2026}.  Other positive results impose interval or related
preference structure~\cite{pierczynski2022}.  Berker et al.~\cite{berker2026}
give an exact mixed-integer/duality framework and additional special cases.

Our restriction is orthogonal to these bounds.  Write
\[
  H_i=C\setminus A_i
\]
for voter $i$'s disapproval set.  We assume only $|H_i|\le2$.  Thus the
number of voter types may grow without bound, and the result applies in the
open parameter range, for example at $(m,k)=(17,9)$ when every voter approves
at least fifteen candidates.  A targeted primary-source audit using
``bounded disapproval,'' ``two-veto,'' ``dense approvals,'' and
``complement rank'' terminology found no equivalent theorem; the novelty
conclusion is necessarily bounded and is not a claim of absolute priority.

\section{Selection rule and main theorem}

The endpoint $m=k$ is immediate by taking $W=C$.  Assume henceforth that
$m>k\ge2$ and put $s=m-k\ge1$.  A committee is represented by its missing
set $B=C\setminus W$, where $|B|=s$.  Define
\[
 e(B)=\sum_{i:\,|H_i|=2} w_i\,\mathbf 1[H_i\cap B\ne\varnothing],
 \qquad
 j(B)=\sum_i w_i|H_i\cap B|.
 \tag{2}
\]
Choose a size-$s$ set $B$ lexicographically maximizing $(e(B),j(B))$ and
return $W=C\setminus B$.

\begin{theorem}[Co-rank-two core theorem]\label{thm:main}
For every $m\ge k\ge2$, every approval election in which $|C\setminus
A_i|\le2$ for every positive-weight voter has a nonempty Hare core.  When
$m>k$, every committee selected by rule~(2) is in the core.
\end{theorem}

The rule is explicit and finite.  We do not claim a polynomial-time
implementation; exhaustive evaluation over all $\binom ms$ missing sets is
sufficient for the mathematical statement and the supplied verifier.

\section{Proof}

Let $T$ be a nonempty target of size $t\le k$.  Write $T=C\setminus D$ and
$d=k-t$.  Then $|D|=s+d$.  Direct cancellation gives, for every voter,
\[
 |A_i\cap T|-|A_i\cap W|
 =-d+|H_i\cap D|-|H_i\cap B|.
 \tag{3}
\]

\subsection{Targets of size at most $k-2$}

If $d\ge2$, the right side of (3) is at most $-d+|H_i|\le0$.  No voter
strictly gains, so such a target cannot block.

\subsection{Targets of size $k-1$}

Now $d=1$.  Strict gain in (3) requires
\[
 |H_i\cap D|-|H_i\cap B|\ge2.
\]
Thus every gainer has $|H_i|=2$, has $H_i\subseteq D$, and has
$H_i\cap B=\varnothing$.  In particular, every gainer is a two-disapproval
voter not counted by $e(B)$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:prob}
A uniformly random size-$s$ set meets any fixed two-set with probability
\[
 p_{m,s}=1-\frac{k(k-1)}{m(m-1)}>\frac1k.
 \tag{4}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The avoidance probability is
$\binom{m-2}{s}/\binom ms=k(k-1)/(m(m-1))$.  With $m=k+s$,
\[
 k[m(m-1)-k(k-1)]-m(m-1)
 =(k-1)((2s-1)k+s(s-1))>0,
\]
which is exactly the strict inequality in (4).
\end{proof}

Let $w_2$ be the total weight of two-disapproval voters.  Averaging (2) over
random $B'$ and using maximality gives $e(B)\ge p_{m,s}w_2$.  Hence every
size-$(k-1)$ target has gainer weight at most
\[
 w_2-e(B)\le(1-p_{m,s})w_2<1-\frac1k=\frac{k-1}{k}.
\]
This is strictly below its Hare quota.

\subsection{Targets of size $k$}

Here $d=0$, so blocking requires every positive-weight voter to gain.  Suppose
that happened for some other size-$s$ missing set $D$.  Equation (3) yields
\[
 |H_i\cap D|>|H_i\cap B| \qquad\text{for every }i.
 \tag{5}
\]
An empty $H_i$ makes (5) impossible.  For each two-disapproval voter, (5)
implies that every pair hit by $B$ is also hit by $D$, and every pair missed
by $B$ is hit by $D$.  Therefore $e(D)\ge e(B)$.  Strict inequality
contradicts the first lexicographic objective.  If equality holds, summing
(5) over all voters gives $j(D)>j(B)$, contradicting the tie-break.  Thus no
size-$k$ target blocks.  Together with the two preceding cases this proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Exact verification}

The primary verifier represents disapproval types by immutable sets and
anonymous count vectors.  On its sealed grid it checks 101,851
profile--parameter cases, 236,801 tied lexicographic maximizers, and
12,020,946 literal committee--target inequalities.  The independent verifier
uses bitmasks and a separate enumeration architecture; it checks 106,620
cases, 265,371 maximizers, and 15,499,790 literal inequalities, including the
boundary $(m,k)=(3,2)$.  Both construct every tied maximizer, not merely one.

All arithmetic is integral after clearing rational voter denominators.  The
finite grids are regression evidence.  Lemma~\ref{lem:prob} and the proof
above establish the unbounded quantifiers.

\section{Scope and open boundary}

The theorem permits arbitrary positive rational weights, repeated ballots,
unapproved candidates, and targets overlapping the committee.  Equality in
the Hare quota counts as blocking throughout.  The theorem does not cover a
voter disapproving three candidates, does not resolve co-rank three, and does
not imply unrestricted core nonemptiness.  The natural next question is
whether a higher-order coverage objective can control deficit-one targets
without losing the equal-size tie-break; the present averaging proof alone
does not supply such an extension.
